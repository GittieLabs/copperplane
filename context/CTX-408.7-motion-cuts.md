# CTX-408.7: Three Cuts That Point At The Finding

> **Spec Reference:** Links to
> [SPEC-408](../specs/SPEC-408-messaging-for-the-maker-who-is-leveling-up.md)

SPEC-408 is `In-Progress` for one reason: the video has not been recorded. This context records
it, but not as one 75-second screen recording. The maintainer asked for **15s, 30s and ~60s cuts
for viewers with short attention spans, with captions and no sound**, and asked that they **pull the
finding out of the UI** rather than wait for the viewer to spot a warning in a full window.

The technique is adapted from a public write-up of the Opus 5.5 motion-design pipeline. The post's
own claim is "the prompt is 10% of the video, the other 90% is the harness". Kept from it:

- a render that is a pure function of time,
- closed-form springs,
- a beat-by-beat shot list instead of a mood,
- **real product UI only, cropped, never redrawn**,
- a critique loop where the agent looks at its own frames.

Dropped: music, beat detection, synthesized sound and voice.

**Depended on #472, merged as `435a247`.** That PR carries the script's "shoot it against a
build" and segment-5 notes. It is now merged into this branch.

### Phase 2: Capture harness — the agent drives the real app

Proven feasible on 2026-10-10 (Section 4), with these constraints:

*   [x] Shoot against a production build (`tauri build --bundles app`), launched by full path. Never
      `tauri dev`. Use a signed release instead if one with the SPEC-340 and SPEC-343 fixes exists
      by shoot day.
*   [x] **Stills:** `screencapture -x -o -l<windowid>`, wrapped in `scripts/capture_still.py`.
      Each still is recorded in the session manifest with its SHA-256, pixel size and the
      build on screen: core build time and sidecar SHA-1. That is what lets Phase 3 trace a
      caption number back to a specific capture of a specific build. This captures the window even when covered,
      at 2560×1800, ~5 fps. Most shots are stills, because the pullouts add the motion.
*   [x] **Motion takes** (the 3D enclosure, a live scroll, KiCad's DRC dialog) use the `cap-cli`
      dev build. It records a screen region, so each take needs Copperplane in front and nobody
      using the screen. Keep takes short, and announce each one to the maintainer.
*   [x] Run a fixed session order:
      1. Launch the build.
      2. The maintainer clears the keychain prompts and says go.
      3. Lock the window size.
      4. Capture the stills.
      5. Record the motion takes.
      6. Contact-sheet every clip before closing the app.
*   [x] **Waiting on LLM calls:** run each "Run Review" once off camera; the saved review then loads
      instantly on camera. The board check's summary is one LLM call: capture before, progress,
      after, and cut the wait to a ~0.5s beat. Never show a result the app did not produce.
*   [x] Clear `.kicad_dru` before segments 2, 4 and 5, and do not rebuild between them (script,
      after #472).
*   [x] **Decided 2026-10-10: shoot the app in light mode, and set it on a dark background in the
      video.**
      - Pullouts lift a card with a drop shadow. Shadows read on light and vanish on dark, where
        the card would need a glow, which is a banned look.
      - KiCad's black board editor (segments 1 and 2) cutting to a light Copperplane shows "hard
        thing, then help" before any caption is read.
      - Thin light text on dark, and orange on dark worst of all, smears under platform
        re-compression at phone size.
      - Cost: the video no longer matches the dark screenshots in the docs.
*   [x] **Before any PCB or Review shot, re-run every saved result on the build being filmed.**
      The probe found the PCB tab showing a result saved 16 days earlier: "Against KiCad's own
      defaults" above **16 violations at 0.15 mm** and the `copperplane-verification-canary` rule.
      That is the exact wrong number SPEC-340 fixed. A fresh build displays old saved results until
      **Check again** is pressed, and the stale one carries a "Checked 16 days ago" stamp that
      would itself be in the shot.
*   [x] Hide Cap's rendered cursor in the export settings for segment 3, or park the cursor
      outside the window. **Resolved without either:** Cap stores the pointer separately

      any export.) Cap draws the pointer into the video, and segment 3's claim is that

*   [x] **Every number in a caption is read off its source frame.** The entry carries that frame,

### Phase 4: Compositor

*   [x] The pullout beat, in four steps:

## 4. Deviations & Discoveries (Plan Drift)

### After Phase 5: the faded highlight blinks back (2026-10-16)

*   **My round 2 fix shipped a defect of its own, and every cut carries it.** I made the old
    highlight fade at a pan with `1 - spring(SNAPPY)`. I predicted a smooth fade. Instead it blinks
    back at full strength four times over 2.5 s.
    - `SNAPPY` is under-damped. The spring overshoots 1, so `1 - spring` goes negative.
    - A canvas ignores a negative `globalAlpha` and keeps the last value, 1.
    - The spring rings, so this happens in four lobes, each about 0.32 s long. Rigovera's port
      reported one flash at about 1.1 s. That was the second lobe.
*   **The motion strips this context required did not catch it.** The log records no strip of
    this handoff taken after the fade went in. Rigovera's port found it, with a strip.
*   **Fix and guard.** `fadeOut()` in `src/motion.js` clamps the value. A test checks it stays in
    [0, 1] and that `SNAPPY` really overshoots, so the test cannot pass vacuously.
*   **Before and after** are motion strips from `sheets.py --strip` (new) at each `continues`
    handoff, plus a frame-by-frame diff of the old and new renders. The diff is non-zero only
    inside the four lobes, so the fix changed nothing else. Details are in
    [`docs/video/review-log.md`](../docs/video/review-log.md) Round 6.
*   **The published cuts carry the old version.** They are the 12 on the `product-video-2026-10`
    release and the two site copies in `docs/site/public/video/` (`CTX-408.9`). None has been
    replaced. That is the maintainer's call.
*   **Not verified by eye:** strips were looked at for the 9:16 renders only. All 12 renders, in
    every format at every handoff, were compared with the old ones numerically. They differ only
    inside the four lobes.
*   **I nearly claimed that check before running it.** My first draft of this entry said the other
    formats "were diffed". The renders had not finished, and the first 4:5 comparison then crashed
    on a frame-size bug in the diff script. The claim was true only after both were fixed and rerun.

### Phase 4: the compositor (2026-10-10)

*   **The renderer is a plain HTML page with `seek(t)`, rendered by `playwright-core` 1.63.0
    driving the installed Chrome. Not Remotion.**
    - Two dependencies (that and `@fontsource/ibm-plex-sans` 5.3.0), each pinned at a release at
      least two weeks old.
    - No browser download.
    - The only hard part, the clip, needs no framework: `prepare.py` extracts its frames to PNG,
      so no browser `<video>` seeking is involved.
    - The data step is Python because the OCR and redaction code already is. The page only draws.
    - Lives in `docs/video/studio/`; its `build/` and `out/` are gitignored, like the captures.
*   **`prepare.py` redacts every base window, then OCRs it again and stops if the path is still
    readable.** The pullout crops are checked path-free by `check_shotlist.py`, but the dimmed
    window behind them is still legible. Confirmed on the pixels: the PCB tab's path line comes
    out pixelated.
*   **Highlights are placed by OCR, not by hand.** Each phrase is matched against the crop's
    OCR'd words, and the build stops below a 70% token match. All 8 highlights were drawn on
    their crops and checked by eye; every one lands on its phrase.
*   **My first render was 406×720, not 1080×1920.**
    - The page's preview CSS shrinks the canvas to the window.
    - Playwright's default viewport is 1280×720.
    - An element screenshot captures the canvas *as displayed*.
    Caught by `ffprobe`, not by looking. The fix: each format renders in a viewport of its own
    size, and the renderer refuses to start if the canvas displays at any other size.
*   **Lifting a card was not enough on a phone. I had predicted it would be.** A 1,792-px card
    fitted to a 1,080-px frame has text as small as the window it came from. The pullout gained
    a fifth beat: once the card lands, the camera pushes in until the highlighted phrase spans
    92% of the frame width. Checked at 360 px wide (the post's phone test): "Its own 5V pin
    (pin 5) is not connected to anything." and "only has 2 pins" both read.
*   **Frames are deterministic, checked rather than assumed.** At four times in the 60 s cut, a
    frame rendered directly, then again after seeking elsewhere, hashes identically. The check
    can fail: three of the four frames half a second later differ. **The fourth does not**, and
    that is real: at 13.9 s, segment 3 has finished moving, and nothing moves for half a second.
    That is the post's "dead beat", for Phase 5.
*   **Rendering:** about 11 frames a second at 1080×1920. The 15 s cut takes 36 s; the 60 s cut
    takes 162 s. All three formats render with no page errors, and every shot kind was exercised
    by the 60 s cut in 16:9.
*   **For Phase 5, from the first contact sheets:**
    - in 16:9 the push-in to 92% of 1,920 px makes text oversized and spills it off both edges;
      the zoom needs a cap based on text height;
    - the zoomed card floats above the middle of the 9:16 frame;
    - captions and pullouts change at different times ("No rule was broken." sits under the
      "2 pins" zoom);
    - the orbit's crop leaves the case small;
    - the side-by-side windows are small in 16:9.

    - The base crop starts below the "Where you are" card, which says "The board has been

    - I had miscounted one caption's words, so segment 4's 30 s slot was 0.12 s short of
      readable.
    - Another caption was 43 characters, one over the phone limit.
    I had done the reading-time arithmetic by hand and believed it.
*   **Every capture check was made to fail on its own defect before I trusted it:**
    - a stale 0.15 mm caption is caught;
    - a crop off the edge of the frame is caught;
    - a crop over the project path is caught.

    - A scene-change pass over take A reported zero changed frames, on footage where the case
      visibly turns.

    printed the same zero for a broken take. Sample by **time** (`fps=N`), and read frame
    timestamps (`-show_entries frame=pts_time`) to measure motion.
*   **Not timed:** how long the board check and the review take. Both finished inside the waits
    used (about 2 min and 75 s), which bounds them but measures nothing.
